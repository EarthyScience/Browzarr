## Exporting static images

Click the image icon (**Export view as Image**) in the navigation bar to open the export settings:

- **Titles** — **Main Title**, **Colorbar Label** and **Colorbar Units** (one of each per variable in bivariate mode)
- **Include Background** and **Include Axis**
- **Include Colorbar** — draws the colorbar and tick labels into the exported image, with **Colorbar Location** (`left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`; corners in bivariate mode) and **Number of Ticks**
- **Set Resolution** — export at a custom **Width** × **Height**; hover **Preview Extent** to see the exported region. Otherwise, **Double Resolution** exports at twice the plot size

Under **Export As**, keep the switch on **Image** and click **Export**. The plot, axes and colorbar are composited off-screen and downloaded as `browzarr-plot.png`.

## Animation

Open **Animation Settings** in the export menu:

- **Frames** and **FPS** — length and frame rate of the video (frames default to the length of the selected time range)
- **Orbit** — rotate the camera during the animation, with **Orbit Degrees**, **Direction** and **Ping-Pong**
- **Animate Time** — step through the time dimension, with **Time FPS** and **Loop Time**
- **Preview** / **Final** — fast low-quality test render vs. full-quality output

Then set the switch under **Export As** to **Animation** and click **Export**. Frames are rendered (including the colorbar) and encoded with ffmpeg in the browser to `browzarr-animation.mp4`, ready for presentations or papers.

## Keyframes

Open the **Keyframe Editor** from **Animation Settings**. Each keyframe captures the *entire current state* (camera, visuals, and other animatable settings) as one snapshot; playback interpolates between them.

- **Add new Keyframe** — record the current state at the current frame (on an existing keyframe, overwrites it)
- **Click a keyframe** — jump to its state; **double-click** — delete it
- **Clear all Keyframes**, **Preview full animation**, and set the number of frames

Recommended workflow: keyframe camera moves first, then change colors, slicing or opacity. Note that **Orbit** overrides keyframed camera motion and **Animate Time** overrides keyframed time changes.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
For reproducible figures, fix the colorbar bounds manually before exporting — otherwise each frame may autoscale its range.
::
